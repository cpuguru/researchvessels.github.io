About ResearchVessels.org

An open, public-interest registry of the world's research platforms, their cruises, where they've been, and where their data lives.

Who runs it

ResearchVessels.org is owned and maintained by Doug White, who developed it at OCEANIC and has maintained it since. A non-profit home for the open-data work is being set up, and oceandata.org may serve as the umbrella for related ocean open-data efforts.

History

The site began in the early 1990s as part of OCEANIC, the Ocean Information Center (oceandata.org), originally hosted at the University of Delaware's College of Marine Studies in Lewes, Delaware. It was built to support the World Ocean Circulation Experiment and later served the Federal Oceanographic Fleet Coordination Council, IOC/IODE national cruise plans, and European portals. Federal agencies, including NSF, NOAA, ONR, the US Coast Guard, and the Naval Oceanographic Office, funded the original work. We gratefully acknowledge that history. This new version is a fresh design.

Principles

  • Free and open, with no login needed to read
  • Findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable (FAIR)
  • Every fact carries its source and the dates it applies to
  • A sparse record is better than no record
  • Links to the archives that hold cruise data (R2R, NCEI, BCO-DMO, ERDDAP) instead of copying them
  • A one-editor reference work, kept as a public compilation rather than a crowd-edited wiki

Data sources in this prototype

  • Master vessel list (2026), merged from the legacy researchvessels.org database, GMSO, VesselConnect, and Wikipedia/Wikidata
  • Legacy researchvessels.org cruise schedules, 1990-2015
  • Historical positions from Sailwx.info (Hal Mueller), 2003-2014, used here as coarse daily positions for 2008-2014
  • Port locations from the World Port Index (Pub. 150), National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, Maritime Safety Information. U.S. government work, public domain. Lewes, Delaware is not in the WPI; the site keeps a project coordinate for it and does not invent a WPI number.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find upcoming cruise schedules, including NOAA's?

This site does not publish future schedules yet. Use the operators' own schedules: UNOLS vessel schedules for the U.S. academic fleet and the NOAA OMAO Ship Finder for NOAA ships. More schedule sites are listed under Other sites with research vessels. Past cruises from the original site (1990-2015) are shown on each vessel's page.

What happened to the original ship pages, photos, and link lists?

The ship records and legacy cruise schedules from the original database are now in the vessel directory. Photos appear on vessel pages where the source and license are known. The old country pages have been retired. The old link lists were checked, and the links that still work are on the Resources page; older technical links and the original data-entry forms are in its Archive.

How do I update my ship's information?

Use the For operators page to send changes to specs, contacts, schedules, and on-board equipment. The editor reviews every change before it appears; nothing is published automatically. Questions: ships@oceandata.org.

Why are the pages so plain?

For the same reason as in the 1990s: many users, including people on ships using satellite links, have slow or costly connections. Pages stay light, need no login, and load their data from small static files.

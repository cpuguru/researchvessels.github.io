About ResearchVessels.org

An open, public-interest registry of the world's research platforms, their cruises, where they've been, and where their data lives.

ResearchVessels.org is an independent project of Doug White (CPU Guru LLC). He continues to own the domain and the compiled registry, independently of the University of Delaware. This prototype is a re-imagining of that long-running Research Vessels project after its federal funding ended. A non-profit home for the open-data work is being set up, and oceandata.org may serve as the umbrella for related ocean open-data efforts.

History

The research vessels schedules and information project began in the early 1990s as a WOCE Data and Information Unit (DIU) spin-off, originally hosted at the University of Delaware's College of Marine Studies (now the College of Earth, Ocean, and Environment) in Lewes, Delaware, as part of OCEANIC, the Ocean Information Center (oceandata.org). Development started before Doug White came on board at the University. He took the project over when the previous Co-PI left, and he was the PI on the subsequent grants that supported it. It later served the Federal Oceanographic Fleet Coordination Council, IOC/IODE national cruise plans, and European portals. Federal agencies, including NSF, NOAA, ONR, the US Coast Guard, and the Naval Oceanographic Office, funded the original work. We gratefully acknowledge that history. After funding ended, the project continued independently. This new version is a prototype redesign.

Principles

  • Free and open, with no login needed to read
  • Findable, accessible, interoperable, reusable (FAIR)
  • Every fact carries its source and the dates it applies to
  • A sparse record is better than no record
  • Links to the archives that hold cruise data (R2R, NCEI, BCO-DMO, ERDDAP) instead of copying them
  • A one-editor reference work, kept as a public compilation rather than a crowd-edited wiki

Data sources in this prototype

  • Master vessel list (2026), merged from the legacy researchvessels.org database, GMSO, VesselConnect, and Wikipedia/Wikidata
  • Legacy researchvessels.org cruise schedules, 1990-2015
  • Historical positions from Sailwx.info (Hal Mueller), 2003-2014, used here as coarse daily positions for 2008-2014
  • Port locations from the World Port Index (Pub. 150), National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency, Maritime Safety Information. U.S. government work, public domain. Lewes, Delaware is not in the WPI; the site keeps a project coordinate for it and does not invent a WPI number.

Governance: who maintains it, corrections and verification

Draft, 2026-11-27. This section says how the registry is run today. It will be updated as the project grows.

Who maintains the site

ResearchVessels.org is maintained by Doug White through CPU Guru LLC as an independent project. It is not run by, or on behalf of, any university, agency, ship operator or other registry. Sources such as R2R, NOAA, VesselConnect and GMSO are credited where their data is used; using their data does not make them partners in this site.

One editor reviews every change. Nothing is published automatically: forms on this site produce a file or an email for the editor, not a live edit.

How corrections work

  1. Send the correction: operators use the For operators page (ship details and on-board equipment) or the Vehicle Condition Form for uncrewed vehicles. Anyone can email ships@oceandata.org.
  2. The editor checks it against a source: the operator's own page or document, a fleet register such as R2R, or a dated record of use.
  3. The change goes live with its source and the date it was checked. The old value is kept in the record's history where the schema allows it.

An operator's confirmation is shown as operator-verified. Everything else is compiled from public sources.

Verification and what the statuses mean

Ships and platforms use the same status labels. Each label is our reading of dated public evidence, not an official statement by the operator. Every vessel and platform page shows the basis for its status, the date it was checked and the source.

Active
Used or published within about 12 months of the last check (for example a cruise in the R2R catalog or a dated expedition).
Listed
Listed as current on the operator's own site or in a fleet register such as R2R; no dated use checked yet.
Probable
Seen in use 2021-2025, nothing newer found.
Dormant
The data trail stopped; no use found for several years.
Unconfirmed
Not yet verified. Most ship records come from the legacy registry and start here. They are shown as unverified, not as active, until a current source is checked.
Retired
Reported retired, lost, replaced or out of service, or its program ended.

A record moves off Unconfirmed only on positive evidence. A ship missing from a list is not marked retired for that reason alone.

Advisory partners

An advisory group of vessel operators, data centres and users is planned, to review the verification rules and the status labels. Pending: no advisory partners have been confirmed yet, and none will be named here until they agree.

Merged and renamed records

When two records turn out to describe the same ship or vehicle, one is kept and the other ID becomes an alias. Old links still work and open the kept record. For example, the deep-sea vehicles Alvin, Jason and Sentry are now single platform records, and their old vessel-directory IDs redirect to them.

Frequently asked questions

Where can I find upcoming cruise schedules, including NOAA's?

This site does not publish future schedules yet. Use the operators' own schedules: UNOLS vessel schedules for the U.S. academic fleet and the NOAA OMAO Ship Finder for NOAA ships. More schedule sites are listed under Other sites with research vessels. Past cruises from the original site (1990-2015) are shown on each vessel's page.

What happened to the original ship pages, photos, and link lists?

The ship records and legacy cruise schedules from the original database are now in the vessel directory. Photos appear on vessel pages where the source and license are known. The old country pages have been retired. The old link lists were checked, and the links that still work are on the Resources page; older technical links and the original data-entry forms are in its Archive.

How do I update my ship's information?

Use the For operators page to send changes to specs, contacts, schedules, and on-board equipment. The editor reviews every change before it appears; nothing is published automatically. Questions: ships@oceandata.org.

Why are the pages so plain?

For the same reason as in the 1990s: many users, including people on ships using satellite links, have slow or costly connections. Pages stay light, need no login, and load their data from small static files.

Can I reuse the data?

Yes. The compiled registry is licensed under CC BY 4.0 (see License and data credits). Please credit: Data compiled by Doug White / researchvessels.org. Each fact carries its source, so please also credit the original source where one is shown. Photographs and some upstream datasets have their own licenses. The site code is all rights reserved.
